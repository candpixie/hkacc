We study the work

Hong Kong film, literature, Cantopop lyricism, comics, and the Cantonese language. Taken apart and made, not just celebrated.

Hong Kong produced one of the most influential film industries of the twentieth century, a literary tradition that barely reaches an American syllabus, a comics culture with its own grammar, and a pop songwriting practice built on a constraint almost no other language imposes. None of it is taught here.

HKACC is a study club. We pick the work apart, bring in the people who made it, and make things ourselves. Everything runs in Cantonese and English side by side, so the door is not only open to people who already speak it.

The first year is deliberately small and specific: a reading circle that actually meets, one screening series, a handful of guests worth flying a question at, and a night in spring where members show what they made.

讀Read Hong Kong literature, comics, and criticism, in Cantonese and English side by side.
睇Watch New Wave, post-handover independent film, martial arts cinema treated as a craft tradition.
做Make Lyrics, zines, translations, subtitles. Members leave the year with work, not just notes.

啟事 · Notice

Screenings run with student or faculty introductions. Hong Kong guests appear by video in the New York morning, which is their evening. Workshops are bilingual and assume no Cantonese.

Most members will not be Hong Kong students. That is the design, not a concession. Open to every enrolled NYU student, every school.

In person · workshop

Cantopop Lyrics Lab

Writing in Tone

Two hours on the problem in the plate above. Three songs, the constraint heard working, then groups write and perform lyrics over a supplied melody. Bilingual worksheets. Native speakers and learners paired on purpose. A working Hong Kong lyricist takes the last half hour to pull the results apart.

Online · recurring

Studio Visit

Hong Kong Artists at Work

An hour with a working artist, illustrator, or filmmaker, set in the New York morning so it lands in their evening. Work in progress, shown and explained. The second half belongs to students, who bring their own for criticism. Recorded with permission, so the hour keeps working after it ends.
